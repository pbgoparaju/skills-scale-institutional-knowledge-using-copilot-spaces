# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview

OctoAcme follows a structured, customer-first project management approach designed to deliver iterative increments, maintain clear ownership, and make data-informed decisions. Our processes emphasize psychological safety, transparency, and continuous improvement.

The OctoAcme process is built around a disciplined lifecycle: **initiation**, **planning**, **execution**, **release**, and **close/retrospective**. Each phase has clear deliverables, checkpoints, and artifacts that ensure projects move from idea to shipped outcome while maintaining stakeholder alignment and accountability. The approach combines rigorous planning with iterative delivery, allowing teams to adapt as they learn while staying accountable to business outcomes.

At its core, OctoAcme relies on clearly defined roles and responsibilities. **Product Managers** define problems, prioritize roadmaps, and measure impact; **Project Managers** coordinate timing, dependencies, risks, and stakeholder communication; **Developers** build and validate features; **QA/Testing** ensures quality and acceptance criteria are met. This distributed ownership model works because all roles operate within common standards: acceptance criteria, definition of done, project artifacts (charter, backlog, risk register, release plan), and structured communication cadences.

Communication is foundational to OctoAcme's success. The organization expects regular syncs at multiple levels—daily standups for delivery teams, weekly PM and product lead alignment, stakeholder updates on a regular cadence, and demos at sprint or milestone boundaries. Equally important is the formalization of escalation paths for blockers and risks (team-level → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor) and the use of a single source of truth (project docs, project boards, release notes) to keep everyone aligned on status, decisions, and dependencies.

Quality assurance and delivery discipline are embedded throughout. Teams define acceptance criteria and a definition of done before work is considered complete, then verify it through CI, tests, security scans, and manual QA where appropriate. Release and deployment gates add further rigor, including smoke testing, rollback planning, and post-deploy verification. Finally, retrospectives turn learning into action: process improvements are tracked, assigned, and reviewed so the team continuously evolves its practices.

## Key Principles
- **Customer-first** — Prioritize customer value and usability
- **Iterative delivery** — Deliver small, testable increments
- **Clear ownership** — Named PM and Product Lead on every project
- **Data-informed** — Measure impact and iterate based on evidence
- **Psychological safety** — Encourage feedback and learning

## Communication Cadence
- **Daily**: Standups (15 min focus on progress, blockers, dependencies)
- **Weekly**: PM + PdM sync and delivery team standups
- **Monthly**: Stakeholder updates
- **Ad-hoc**: Risk escalations and incident management
